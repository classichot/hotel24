Six Thai faces to sit under Archivo

Archivo carries no Thai glyphs, so today every Thai string in HOTEL24 is being rendered by whatever the device happens to have — different on a Mac, a Windows PC and an Android phone. Each option below fixes that by declaring Archivo first and a Thai face behind it: Latin keeps the Modernist identity, Thai comes from the named font. Same six strings in every specimen, at the sizes they actually appear.

Three things decide this: loopless vs looped (Modernist is a grotesque — looped Thai reads decorative next to it), whether the face survives 12–13px in table cells, and whether its heaviest weight can hold a 44px headline beside Archivo 800.

What I would pick

2a IBM Plex Sans Thai for HOTEL24. It was drawn as the Thai companion to a grotesque, so its stroke weight, terminals and x-height land almost exactly on Archivo's — the mixed line จองตรง · ฿2,244 reads as one typeface rather than two. It also stays legible at the 11–12.5px the console's sidebar and tables run at, which is where most of these fail.

If the landing page needs more warmth than the console, 2c Sarabun is the honest second: it is the face Thai owners see in government and bank documents every day, so it reads as trustworthy rather than as a startup. Do not run two Thai faces in one product — pick one and let weight do the work.
